# Gallery

Every picture here is either a real isotop render or a figure generated by a script in the repository; the
one exception, the mockups of planned views, is labelled. The renders come from
[`scripts/render_wiki_media.py`](https://github.com/tfpickard/isotop/blob/main/scripts/render_wiki_media.py),
which regenerates them from the release binary, and the figures from `docs/figures/*.py`
(stdlib-only, deterministic). Nothing is retouched.

![All eighteen views](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tfpickard/isotop/main/docs/media/renders/contact-sheet.webp)

## History

Showing motion as a row of stills is older than cinema. In 1878 Eadweard Muybridge, commissioned by Leland Stanford
to settle whether a galloping horse ever has all four hooves off the ground, lined a track at Stanford's Palo Alto
farm with cameras that the horse tripped as it passed, and published the sequence as *The Horse in Motion*; the
answer was yes. In 1882 the French physiologist Étienne-Jules Marey built a camera shaped like a rifle that took a
series of exposures on one plate. Both made the same discovery that the filmstrips and loops here rest on: a
movement too fast, or too slow, to read as it happens becomes legible as a row.

The gallery contains hens, fish, crabs and starlings. It contains no horse.

## How these were made

* **Stills** are headless demo renders: `isotop --demo --view <name> --renderer cpu --width 1600 --time <t> --output <file>`.
  The demo workload is synthetic and deterministic, so the same command gives the same pixels
  ([Determinism](Theory-Determinism), [Headless and reproducible renders](Guide-Headless-and-Reproducible-Renders)).
  Each caption gives the `--time` used, in demo seconds.
* **Loops** are 50 consecutive frames at 10 fps, 960 by 540. A headless run draws one frame, so a loop is built
  from `--benchmark N --output` (the last of N consecutive frames) to keep one continuous trajectory of the
  simulation with the demo snapshot held fixed. They are not seamless: the last frame jumps back to the first,
  and slow views (Ptolemy, Coop) barely move in five seconds.
* There is no Necropolis loop on purpose: a headless Necropolis frame is static apart from drifting fog.

## History building up

Views that read a minute of history fill in as the session runs. A headless render always replays the warm-up
from before time 0, so for some views the strip is a sliding window rather than a build-up; each caption says
which.

**[Ptolemy](View-Ptolemy)** at 15, 30, 45 and 60 s: stars first, then planets with epicycle trails.

![Ptolemy filmstrip](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tfpickard/isotop/main/docs/media/renders/filmstrip-ptolemy.webp)

**[Tesseract](View-Tesseract)** at 10, 25, 40 and 60 s: the worldlines lengthen. The replayed window saturates
at 60 s although the recorder keeps 180 s.

![Tesseract filmstrip](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tfpickard/isotop/main/docs/media/renders/filmstrip-tesseract.webp)

**[Strata](View-Strata)** at 10, 30, 60 and 120 s. Its warm-up replays 60 s from before time 0, so the history is
already full at every time here: this is a sliding window, not a build-up.

![Strata filmstrip](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tfpickard/isotop/main/docs/media/renders/filmstrip-strata.webp)

**[Necropolis](View-Necropolis)** across a lunation, at 0, 8.6, 16.0 and 23.4 days (thin crescent, first quarter,
full, last quarter), all at zero fog pressure. It replays 600 s from before time 0, so there is no build-up to show.

![Necropolis filmstrip](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tfpickard/isotop/main/docs/media/renders/filmstrip-necropolis.webp)
