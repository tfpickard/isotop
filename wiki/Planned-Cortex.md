# Planned: Cortex

> **Planned, not yet implemented.** Specified in [docs/briefs/views-2026-10.md](https://github.com/tfpickard/isotop/blob/main/docs/briefs/views-2026-10.md) and tracked in issue [#52](https://github.com/tfpickard/isotop/issues/52).

A dark slice of cortex in layers, in which every process is a spiking neuron: its kind sets the layer and the firing type, its CPU sets the input current, its threads are dendrites, and its socket and pipe links are axons along which real, delayed spikes travel as pulses. Somas flash when they fire; talking processes pull each other into step; a raster plot along the bottom shows the last ten seconds of spikes like an electrophysiology rig, and the status line reports Golomb's synchrony measure $\chi$. This page is the design of that view; the model itself is derived in [Math-Spiking-Neurons](Math-Spiking-Neurons).

*`docs/mockups/cortex.jpg`, rendered by the Python prototype `docs/mockups/code/cortex.py` with a real Izhikevich simulation over a mocked process table (the burst of web requests at t = 3 s is mocked). A visual target, not an isotop render.*

## 1. The system being modelled

The neocortex is a six-layered sheet of neurons that talk by action potentials, brief all-or-nothing voltage spikes of about a millisecond, conducted along axons and delivered across synapses after a delay. Individual neurons differ in how they turn a steady input current into a spike train: some fire regularly and slow down (regular spiking), some fire in bursts, some fire fast and without adaptation. A recording rig shows this as a raster, rows being neurons and dots being spikes, and as membrane-potential traces.

The metaphor for a machine is direct: a process is a cell with an input drive (CPU), many short-lived sub-units (threads), and connections to other cells (sockets and pipes) that carry events. The loose point is that "busy processes that talk synchronise", which is both what coupled spiking neurons do and what request-handling services do under load. The honest limit: a neuron's spikes are generated by a model equation fed by CPU, not by anything the process does at spike times; the spikes are a rendering of a smoothed CPU number through a nonlinearity (section 5).

The mathematics (Hodgkin-Huxley, integrate-and-fire, the Izhikevich model and its table of classes, synchrony measures) is on [Math-Spiking-Neurons](Math-Spiking-Neurons). The short version: Izhikevich 2003,

$$
\dot v = 0.04 v^2 + 5 v + 140 - u + I,\qquad \dot u = a(bv - u),\qquad v \ge 30 \Rightarrow v \leftarrow c,\ u \leftarrow u + d,
$$

with milliseconds of time, integrated by the design in 0.5 ms steps.

## 2. Theory specific to this design

### 2.1 Neuron types by kind

| Kind | Type | $(a, b, c, d)$ | Layer band |
|---|---|---|---|
| kernel threads | fast spiking (FS) | (0.1, 0.2, -65, 2) | VI, 20 % of slice height |
| system services | regular spiking (RS) | (0.02, 0.2, -65, 8) | V, 25 % |
| session apps | chattering (CH) | (0.02, 0.2, -50, 2) | II/III, 25 % |
| containers | intrinsically bursting (IB) | (0.02, 0.2, -55, 4) | IV, 18 % |

Layer I (10 %, empty) and white matter (7 %) complete the slice. The pairing of types with layers is by kind and is a convention; real neocortex has fast-spiking cells in every layer and intrinsically bursting cells chiefly in layer V (unverified detail).

### 2.2 Calibrating CPU to current

CPU is a number between 0 and several hundred percent; neurons want a current in the range where the f-I curve does something interesting. The issue's calibration, per type, on first use and **without noise**:

* the rheobase $r_h$ is the smallest $I$ that fires at least 1 Hz;
* $I_{40}$ is the $I$ that fires at 40 Hz;
* the smoothed `cpu / 100` (a fraction of one core) maps piecewise linearly from $[0, 0.005, 0.05, 1, 3]$ to $[0,\ 0.62\,r_h,\ 0.98\,r_h,\ I_{40},\ I_{40} + 2(I_{40} - 0.98\,r_h)]$, clamped beyond 3;
* current noise of $\sigma = 1.2$ per step is added only when $I > 0$.

![CPU to input current to firing rate for the four neuron types](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tfpickard/isotop/main/docs/media/figures/cortex-calibration.svg)

*`docs/figures/cortex-calibration.py` (Izhikevich Euler 0.5 ms, noise seed fixed, 4 s windows after 0.5 s).*

I ran the specified calibration with the stdlib simulator (`docs/figures/izh_w8.py`). Calibrated values: FS $r_h = 3.90$, $I_{40} = 5.15$; RS 3.80 and 19.05; CH 3.80 and 6.51; IB 3.80 and 12.18. Resulting rates with noise:

| CPU % | FS (kernel) | RS (system) | CH (session) | IB (container) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 and 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 5 | 16.5 Hz | 6.3 Hz | 19.5 Hz | 7.0 Hz |
| 10 | 19.5 | 9.5 | 20.5 | 9.8 |
| 50 | 30.5 | 23.3 | 32.5 | 22.5 |
| 100 | 40.3 | 39.3 | 44.0 | 40.8 |
| 300 | 68.3 | 101.5 | 82.3 | 107.5 |

Three consequences, all properties of the *specified constants*, not of any isotop run:

1. **Idle is silent and a full core is about 40 Hz**, as the brief asks, for all four types.
2. **There is no "occasional" regime.** The brief wants about 5 % CPU to fire "occasionally". The map puts 5 % at $0.98\,r_h$, so the neuron sits at the edge of its onset; but the onset of these neurons is abrupt (rate jumps from 0 to roughly 5 to 15 Hz as soon as the 1 Hz criterion is met, see the f-I curves on the maths page), and the current noise pushes the neuron over it. At 5 % CPU the simulated rates are 6 to 20 Hz, not sporadic single spikes. Sporadic firing would need a lower fraction of $r_h$ at 5 % together with a smaller noise.
3. **Above 100 % the types diverge.** FS ends at 68 Hz at 300 %, IB at 108 Hz, because the extrapolated current $I_{40} + 2(I_{40} - 0.98 r_h)$ scales with each type's $I_{40}$.

### 2.3 Synapses and delay

Socket links are excitatory in both directions (the existing `Snapshot.link_traffic` counts both directions together, so a direction cannot be assigned); pipe links run one way, writer to reader. The weight is

$$
w = \min\!\left(0.25 \ln(1 + B/1000),\ 2.0\right), \qquad B = \text{bytes per second},
$$

so $B$ = 1 KB/s gives 0.17, 10 KB/s 0.60, 100 KB/s 1.15, 1 MB/s 1.73, and the cap of 2.0 is reached at $B = 1000(e^8 - 1) \approx 2.98$ MB/s. Where per-link traffic is missing (macOS) it is $\min(0.25\ln(1 + \text{sockets}), 2.0)$, saturating at about 2980 sockets. Synaptic current decays with $\tau = 5$ ms. The conduction delay is the axon's world-space curve length divided by a speed chosen so that **crossing the slice takes 0.8 s**, independent of zoom. That is roughly two orders of magnitude slower than cortical conduction (a few milliseconds across a few millimetres at 1 to 10 m/s): a deliberate time dilation so the viewer can see the pulses, and therefore **decorative in magnitude, real in order**: every pulse drawn is a spike event that was generated, queued with its delay, and will arrive.

### 2.4 Synchrony

Golomb's $\chi^2 = \operatorname{var}_t(\bar V) / \overline{\operatorname{var}_t(V_i)}$ over the "talking" set (neurons with at least one link and at least 5 % CPU), from exponential moving averages with $\tau = 2$ s. For $N$ independent neurons $\chi \approx 1/\sqrt{N}$ and for full synchrony $\chi = 1$ (derivation on the maths page). The inspector also reports $\chi$ for the selected neuron's connected component. In the mockup the "web stack" component reports 0.46 against 0.18 for the busy set, so coupling raises it.

## 3. Mapping

| Visual element | Measured quantity (source) | Transform |
|---|---|---|
| Soma | one process | pickable sphere; grows with `Stage::growth` on birth |
| Layer band | `Process.kind` | band fractions of slice height as in 2.1 |
| Position in band | lifetime seat per kind (`layout::Seats`) | R2 point $(\text{frac}(0.7549\,s), \text{frac}(0.5698\,s))$ in the band plus seeded jitter; slice widens by 1.25x steps and never shrinks |
| Firing type | `Process.kind` | (a, b, c, d) of 2.1 |
| Input current | smoothed `Process.cpu` (EMA $\tau$ 1.5 s) | piecewise map of 2.2 |
| Primary dendrites | `Process.threads` | one per thread, clamped to 1..12; shapes seeded per identity, **decorative** |
| Axon | socket or pipe link in `Snapshot.links` | curve; delay = length / conduction (0.8 s across the slice) |
| Synaptic weight | link traffic (B2; loopback TCP bytes per second today) | $\min(0.25\ln(1+B/1000), 2.0)$ |
| Moving pulse on an axon | a real in-flight spike event | same event queue as the simulation |
| Soma flash | a spike | `Glow` decaying with $\tau = 60$ ms |
| Raster dot | spike time | last 10 s; rows = neurons that fired, ordered by layer then seat; binned when rows exceed half the panel height |
| $\chi$ in the status line | membrane potentials of talking neurons | EMA $\tau$ 2 s |
| Sparkline in inspector | the selected neuron's $v$ | last 320 ms, -80 to +30 mV, 40 braille cells |
| Apical trunk (non-kernel), stain, dust, branch shapes | none | **decorative** |

## 4. Design: how isotop will realise it

* **Module.** `src/cortex.rs`, state struct implementing `views::ViewModule`, one registry row; `Backdrop::Void`, no view-local keys (the global `c` links cycle applies to axons), no digit; `links` rule "drawn by the view". Flat `Vec<f32>` state for $v$ and $u$; at 1000 neurons the worst case is 200 catch-up steps times 1000 neurons, about 200k updates per frame.
* **Clock.** `simulation::FixedStep` with a 0.5 ms step and a 100 ms catch-up cap per frame (200 steps); longer gaps are dropped, so pause freezes the simulation. The neurons run in real time: a 40 Hz neuron fires every 25 ms of wall clock.
* **Warm-up.** A headless warm-up simulates the 10 s before `--time` fed by demo samples once per second, so a PNG's raster is full.
* **Drawing.** Dendrites, axons and raster dots are `Beam`s (never picked); somas are pickable `Sphere`s; at most 3000 pulses are drawn; above 400 neurons, or when a soma is under 3 px, only primary branches are drawn. The raster panel is a screen-space block of triangles over the bottom 22 % of the frame with ASCII `Item::Glyph` labels; a bounds point below the white matter keeps the fitted slice clear of it.
* **Inspector.** Type with $(a,b,c,d)$, $I$ and rate; layer, threads drawn of total; synapses in with minimum and maximum delay; component $\chi$; a two-line braille sparkline recorded only for the selected neuron. The sparkline is sanitised by a **new** function `terminal::clean_sparkline` that passes only U+2800 to U+28FF and space; `terminal::clean_text` stays ASCII-only and still turns braille in a process name into `?`.
* **Birth and exit.** A soma grows on birth (`Stage::growth`); an exited soma shrinks over 1.2 s and leaves 12 seeded dust specks besides the generic flash.
* **Legend.** ` chi 0.18 talking | 594 spikes/s | soma = process, layer by kind | dendrite = thread | axon = link, delay ~ length, weight ~ log traffic | current ~ CPU`.
* **Dependencies** (ROADMAP index): P1, P2, S1, B2 (per-link traffic, pipe direction), S2 (`FixedStep`), S6 (source declaration and wording). At commit `192299b` P1, P2, S1, S2 and S6 are merged; B2's Unix-queue and pipe parts are not (the tree has `Snapshot.link_traffic`, loopback TCP rates summed over both directions, and no `unix_queues` or pipe links yet).
* **Platforms.** Linux: full; if B2 reports traffic unreadable, behave as on macOS. macOS: **available but degraded**: CPU, kind, threads and socket links are sampled but per-link traffic is not (`socket traffic` in `Snapshot.missing`), so the weight is the socket-count formula, and the legend appends ` | no socket traffic: weight = socket count`.
* **No plugin system.** The view is a compile-time module and a `REGISTRY` row.

## 5. Reading the picture

* **Measured:** which processes exist, their kind (layer), their smoothed CPU (through the current map), their thread counts (dendrite count), and which processes share a socket or pipe (axons). Link traffic gives the synaptic weight on Linux.
* **Derived:** the spikes. They are a deterministic function of the smoothed CPU, noise (seeded), and incoming spikes. A spike does **not** mean "the process did something now"; it means the model neuron crossed its threshold. Two processes at the same CPU fire at different times because of their noise seeds.
* **Real in order, decorative in speed:** pulses travel along axons after the delay computed from length; the 0.8 s slice crossing time is a display choice.
* **Resolution limits.** Raster: the panel is about the frame width wide for 10 s, so at 1080p (assuming of the order of 1700 px, the issue fixes only the 22 % height) one pixel is about 6 ms: a chattering burst with 2.5 to 4.5 ms intervals is one tick, a 100 Hz neuron is a solid bar. Flashes: a 60 fps frame is 16.7 ms and the flash decays over 60 ms, so a 40 Hz neuron (25 ms period) flashes steadily rather than visibly pulsing; individual spikes above 30 Hz cannot be resolved by eye on screen.
* **Cannot infer:** CPU from the firing rate (RS, CH and FS give different rates for the same CPU; section 2.2); causal influence between processes (every link is excitatory in both directions, and an axon only shows who is connected); any real neuroscience.
* **Aliasing.** The membrane state is simulated at 2 kHz and sampled by the frame loop at 60 Hz or lower for drawing; the sparkline is read from the state, not from frames.

## 8. Variations and open questions

* Fix the "occasional firing at 5 %" target: either lower the fraction of rheobase at 5 % or reduce the noise; then re-measure with `cortex-calibration.py`.
* Add an inhibitory population (kernel threads as FS interneurons with negative weights, which is their biological role) and see whether $\chi$ becomes more discriminating.
* Show the actual synchrony: compare $\chi$ of a connected component against shuffled links (a null model).
* Map memory to soma size or to the number of synapses instead of leaving it unused.
* Let process priority (`nice`, `Process.priority`) set the resting potential.
